Experience that helps me understand the realities of leadership.

My career has taken me from clinical and scientific work into senior leadership, with responsibility for people, programs, partnerships, and difficult decisions.

My career began in nursing more than three decades ago.

Along the way, I served in the U.S. Air Force and Peace Corps, worked at the National Institutes of Health (NIH), and spent more than two decades at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), including six years as Chief of the Field Support Branch in the Division of Reproductive Health. I have worked alongside and led physicians, nurses, scientists, epidemiologists, researchers, and other highly trained professionals.

Those experiences gave me a deep familiarity with the cultures of public health and healthcare—and with what happens when people who are highly accomplished in their field take on broader responsibility for people, priorities, relationships, and decisions.

I also value the sense of mission and camaraderie I experienced in public service. Much of my career has been spent with people who care deeply about their work, their colleagues, and the communities they serve.

From expertise to leadership

My clinical background continues to shape how I work. I understand the professional identity that can come with being a clinician, scientist, or technical expert—and how different leadership can feel when success depends less on your own expertise and more on what you help others accomplish.

My own career followed that path.

Over time, my responsibilities expanded from clinical and scientific work to leading people, programs, partnerships, and teams; setting priorities; making difficult decisions; and developing other leaders.

I have also been in senior roles where there was no obvious person to talk to about a difficult situation. Those experiences help me understand many of the pressures leaders bring to coaching.

Developing people has always mattered to me

One part of leadership I have valued most is helping people grow—watching them take on greater responsibility, develop confidence in their own judgment, and succeed in roles that ask more of them.

Coaching is a natural extension of that work.

I bring my experience to the conversation, but I do not assume that what worked for me will work for you. My role is to listen, ask questions, challenge assumptions, and help you think through your own situation.

The perspective I bring

My background spans clinical nursing, epidemiology and public health research, federal and public service, leadership of people, programs, and partnerships, teaching and mentoring, and executive and leadership coaching.

My deepest experience is in public health and public service, with clinical roots and leadership experience that translate well to healthcare, academic, and other mission-driven settings.

I am comfortable working with highly trained professionals—including physicians, nurses, scientists, researchers, and other technical experts—whose leadership responsibilities have grown beyond the expertise that first established their careers.
