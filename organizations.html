When coaching may be useful

Coaching may be useful when a valued leader is:

  • Taking on a larger team, program, or portfolio
  • Setting priorities across competing demands
  • Delegating more effectively and developing others
  • Handling difficult relationships or conversations
  • Making decisions when the right path is not obvious
  • Being asked to take greater ownership and work more independently

These are often development challenges, not performance failures.

What an organization may notice

Depending on the leader’s goals and circumstances, coaching may support changes a sponsor can observe, such as:

  • Taking clearer ownership of decisions and next steps
  • Making appropriate decisions with less need for repeated confirmation from senior leaders
  • Delegating work that does not need to stay with the leader
  • Handling difficult conversations more directly and constructively
  • Communicating priorities and recommendations more clearly
  • Showing greater readiness for expanded responsibility

These are examples of developmental shifts, not guaranteed outcomes.

Why one-to-one coaching

Leadership development is most useful when it connects directly to the work a leader is actually doing. One-to-one coaching gives the leader protected time to examine current decisions, relationships, priorities, and patterns while continuing to lead.

How organization-sponsored coaching works

  1. 01

    Align

    The leader, organizational sponsor, and coach agree on the broad purpose and desired outcomes of the engagement.

  2. 02

    Coach

    The leader develops the specific coaching goals and owns the coaching work. Private session content remains confidential within the boundaries established in the coaching agreement.

  3. 03

    Review

    Progress can be discussed against agreed goals. The sponsor receives only the progress information agreed among the leader, sponsor, and coach; private coaching conversations are not reported back.

Standard engagement: The base one-to-one engagement is six virtual sessions over approximately three to four months. See the Coaching page for what is included.

Additional organizational scope, such as extra sponsor meetings, assessments, reporting, or administrative requirements, is scoped separately.

Evidence

Research reviews and meta-analyses generally find positive effects of workplace and executive coaching on learning and development, performance-related outcomes, and a range of leadership and personal outcomes. The evidence base is positive but heterogeneous; findings vary by study design, outcome, and context, and coaching does not guarantee a particular result.

Jones, Woods & Guillaume (2016)de Haan & Nilsson (2023)
